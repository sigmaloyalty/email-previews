AI assistants have quietly become part of everyday life. Many of us use them to draft emails, summarize documents, plan trips or make sense of a confusing bill. They’re genuinely useful, which is exactly why Data Privacy Week (January 25–31) is a good moment to ask a simple question: what have I been telling mine?


A chat feels like a private conversation, but it’s information you’ve handed to a service. What happens to it depends on the tool, the type of account and settings most of us have never opened. That’s worth knowing before you type. The good news is that you don’t need to give up the tools. A few habits do most of the work.


What Doesn’t Belong in the Chat


Before you paste or upload, ask yourself whether you’d be comfortable seeing it on a stranger’s screen. Keep these out:


  • Your SIN, passport, or driver’s licence details, including photos or scans of your ID
  • Banking details: card and account numbers, passwords and one-time codes
  • Medical records and test results
  • Other people’s information: a friend’s address, a client’s file, a coworker’s details. It isn’t yours to share.


Documents deserve extra care. When you upload a statement, contract or form, you’re sharing everything on it, not only the part you’re asking about. Before you upload anything, be sure to remove or black out sensitive details, or swap in placeholders like [NAME] and [ACCOUNT] in digital documents, and ask for the help you need without exposing your personal information.

Run a Privacy Audit on Your AI Tools


Last January we encouraged a privacy audit of your apps and accounts: review the settings, then decide. The same habit applies here. For the assistant you use most, look for:


  • Chat history and training. Many assistants let you choose whether conversations are saved and whether they can be used to improve the service. Find the setting and make a deliberate choice.
  • Memory. Some assistants remember details between conversations. Review what’s stored and delete anything you don’t want kept.
  • Temporary chat modes. Many tools offer a chat that isn’t saved, handy for one-off questions.


Setting names differ from tool to tool and change often, so look under privacy or data controls, and check the tool’s privacy policy for how long conversations are kept.


When Your Assistant Connects to Your Accounts


Assistants increasingly offer to connect to your email, calendar or cloud storage, and even to act for you by booking, sending or buying. That’s convenient, but you’re granting access to everything in that account. Before you say yes:


  • What will it be able to see? Connecting your whole inbox to summarize one email gives the tool far more than the job requires. Connect only what the task needs.
  • Can it only read, or also send, delete or spend? An assistant that can only read is a smaller risk than one that can send messages, delete files or make purchases for you. Start with the narrowest permission available.
  • Can you disconnect later? Check that you can revoke access, in the assistant and in the account you connected. Review your connected apps from time to time, and remove the ones you no longer use.

A Safety Net if Something Slips


Even careful people occasionally share something they later wish they hadn’t, and sometimes personal information leaks from somewhere else entirely. That’s where Securizone Dark Web Monitoring helps. Privacy isn’t a one-time task. A few minutes spent on the AI tools you use most is a good habit to start this Data Privacy Week.


Remember to stay vigilant, stay informed, and stay safe.

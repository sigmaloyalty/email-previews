DATA LEAK ALERT:

WHATSAPP USER DATA EXPOSURE

Hi {{contact.FIRSTNAME}},


We're writing to make you aware of a significant data exposure affecting WhatsApp users worldwide. In November 2025, cybersecurity researchers from the University of Vienna disclosed a vulnerability in WhatsApp's account verification process that allowed them to identify approximately 3.5 billion active WhatsApp accounts — without hacking any servers or accessing private messages. The flaw has since been patched by Meta, WhatsApp's parent company.


If your phone number was detected as part of this exposure, you may have already received a dark web monitoring alert from Securizone. This email is to provide you with additional information about what was exposed, how criminals may use this data to target individuals, and how your Securizone subscription can help you respond and stay protected.

What was exposed?

The researchers who discovered the vulnerability were able to confirm which phone numbers are linked to active WhatsApp accounts, and in many cases retrieve additional public profile information. Specifically, the exposure may have included:


  • Phone numbers confirmed as active on WhatsApp

  • Profile photos for approximately 57% of users whose profiles were set to public

  • "About" status text for approximately 29% of users


Your private messages, contacts list, and financial information were not exposed. Meta has confirmed that end-to-end encrypted chat content was never accessible. However, the publicly available profile data that was exposed is enough for scammers to launch targeted, convincing attacks — particularly against individuals whose phone number, name, and photo can now be linked together.

What should you do to protect yourself?

We recommend taking the following steps to help protect your information and guard against fraud:

  1. Lock down your WhatsApp privacy settings: Open WhatsApp and go to Settings > Privacy. Change your Profile Photo, About, and Last Seen settings to "My Contacts" (or "Nobody") so that strangers can no longer view them. Also restrict who can add you to group chats to help prevent you being added to spam or scam groups.

  2. Enable two-step verification on WhatsApp: Go to Settings > Account > Two-step verification and set a 6-digit PIN. This prevents someone from taking over your WhatsApp account even if they obtain your phone number or intercept a verification code.

  3. Be cautious of messages from unknown senders: Scammers may use your phone number and profile photo to impersonate you, or contact you directly through WhatsApp posing as a trusted brand, financial institution, or even a friend. If you receive an unexpected message with a link or an urgent request, do not click — and use WhatsApp's built-in Block and Report feature to flag the sender.

  4. Watch for "friend in need" impersonation scams: With your profile photo and phone number in hand, a scammer can message your contacts from a new number, pretending to be you and claiming an emergency. Let people close to you know to verify through a call or another channel if they receive an unusual message "from you."

  5. Review your accounts and statements: Keep a close eye on your bank and credit card statements for activity you don't recognize. Criminals may use your phone number as a stepping stone to access other accounts that rely on it for password resets or two-factor authentication.
